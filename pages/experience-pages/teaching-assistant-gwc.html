Teaching Assistant

Organization: Girls Who Code Summer Immersion Program • Setting: Virtual • Summer 2024

As a Teaching Assistant for Girls Who Code Summer Immersion, I supported an instructor in maintaining an engaging and accessible virtual classroom environment for 60 students from diverse backgrounds across the world. I taught game development curriculum using p5.js, p5play, and Replit IDE, providing technical guidance, debugging support, and mentorship to emerging programmers.

Overview

Girls Who Code is a nonprofit organization dedicated to closing the gender gap in computing. The Summer Immersion Program is a four-week intensive program for middle school and high school students to learn coding through hands-on projects, particularly focusing on game development and creative expression through code. As a TA for the virtual cohort, I worked with a highly diverse group of 60 students from around the world.

Program Context

Program Focus: Game Development & Creative Coding • Curriculum: p5.js, p5play, JavaScript • Environment: Virtual (Zoom, Replit, Collaborative Tools) • Cohort Size: 60 students • Duration: 4 weeks intensive

Key Responsibilities

  • Classroom Support: Maintained an engaging and accessible virtual learning environment alongside the lead instructor
  • Curriculum Instruction: Taught game development concepts, code structure, debugging techniques, and best practices
  • Technical Guidance: Provided one-on-one help with p5.js, p5play syntax, and Replit IDE features
  • Debugging Support: Helped students troubleshoot code errors, understand error messages, and problem-solve
  • Mentorship: Provided encouragement, answered questions, and helped students overcome frustration with coding challenges
  • Engagement: Used breakout rooms, interactive coding exercises, and creative projects to keep diverse learners engaged
  • Inclusivity: Ensured all students felt welcome and supported, particularly first-time coders and underrepresented groups in tech
  • Assessment: Provided feedback on student projects and progress, celebrating achievements and identifying areas for growth

Learning Outcomes for Students

By the end of the program, students could:

  • Understand fundamental programming concepts (variables, loops, conditionals, functions)
  • Create interactive games using p5.js and p5play
  • Debug code and troubleshoot common programming errors
  • Collaborate with peers on group projects
  • Present their work and articulate their creative choices
  • Develop confidence in their coding abilities
  • Build a portfolio of game projects

Teaching Approach & Strategies

1

Create Psychological Safety

Foster an environment where students feel comfortable making mistakes, asking questions, and trying new things.

2

Meet Students Where They Are

Recognize varying skill levels and provide differentiated support—advanced challenges for quick learners, extra scaffolding for those struggling.

3

Make It Relevant & Fun

Game development is inherently engaging; celebrate student creativity and emphasize that coding is a creative tool.

4

Encourage Peer Learning

Facilitate collaboration through pair programming, peer review, and group projects so students learn from each other.

5

Provide Actionable Feedback

Give specific, constructive feedback: what worked, what could improve, and concrete next steps.

Technical Skills Taught

  • p5.js Fundamentals: Setup, canvas, drawing functions, coordinate systems
  • JavaScript Basics: Variables, data types, conditionals, loops, functions, arrays, objects
  • p5play Library: Sprites, collision detection, animations, game states
  • Game Design Concepts: Game loops, input handling, scoring, win/lose conditions
  • Debugging: Console logging, browser dev tools, error interpretation
  • Best Practices: Code organization, naming conventions, comments, version control basics

Program Impact & Outcomes

  • Student Confidence: Many students shared that this was their first programming experience and they now feel confident continuing to learn
  • Diverse Perspectives: Virtual format enabled participation from students worldwide with different cultural backgrounds and perspectives
  • Creative Expression: Students created impressive games reflecting their interests—from space adventures to social simulations
  • Community Building: Despite virtual setting, students formed connections, collaborated on projects, and supported each other
  • Inspiration: Many students expressed interest in computer science and technology careers

Personal Growth & Learnings

This TA experience was transformative. I learned that teaching is as much about creating an inclusive, supportive environment as it is about technical knowledge. Seeing students' faces light up when they successfully ran their first game, or when they fixed a bug they'd been struggling with, reinforced why I care about technology education. I also gained appreciation for the challenge of teaching virtually and became adept at reading virtual room dynamics and providing effective remote support.

Commitment to Tech Equity

Through Girls Who Code, I witnessed firsthand how women and underrepresented groups are dramatically underrepresented in tech fields. I'm committed to being an ally and advocate for diversity in computing, whether through mentorship, education, hiring, or support for organizations like GWC.

Impact Statement

If even one student in that cohort of 60 goes on to pursue computer science because they had a positive experience and felt supported in their learning journey, then this teaching experience will have been profoundly worthwhile. Tech needs diverse perspectives, and programs like Girls Who Code are essential in building that future.
